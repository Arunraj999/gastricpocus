Managing local anaesthetic toxicity

A summary for teaching. In an emergency follow your hospital guideline and the Association of Anaesthetists quick reference handbook.

20% lipid emulsion: dose by weight

Use lean body weight. Dosing as summarised by El-Boghdadly and colleagues from the ASRA and Association of Anaesthetists guidance.12

1 · Recognise

Think of it early

  • Central nervous system features are the commonest presentation (68 to 77%), usually seizures.1
  • About 40% of cases present atypically and one fifth with cardiovascular signs alone.1
  • Onset is usually soon after injection but can be delayed, particularly with infusions.12
2 · Immediate steps

Stop, call, oxygenate

  • Stop injecting and call for help.1
  • Secure the airway and give 100% oxygen. Hypoxia, hypercapnia and acidosis make toxicity worse.1
  • Give 20% lipid emulsion early in any event judged potentially serious.1
3 · Seizures and circulation

Support, with modified drugs

  • Benzodiazepines are first line for seizures. Avoid large doses of propofol if the circulation is compromised.1
  • In cardiac arrest follow advanced life support, with small initial doses of adrenaline (1 microgram/kg or less).1
  • Avoid vasopressin, and do not use lidocaine as an antiarrhythmic. Amiodarone is first line for ventricular arrhythmia.1
  • If there is no rapid recovery, consider cardiopulmonary bypass early.1
4 · Afterwards

Observe and report

  • Monitor for at least 6 hours after cardiovascular features, and at least 2 hours after brief central nervous system features.1

Prevention

Reducing the risk

Dose

Lowest effective dose

Base the limit on the dosing weight (actual body weight, or lean body weight at a BMI of 40 or more), and reduce it in older, frail or pregnant patients.12

Guidance

Use ultrasound

Ultrasound guidance reduced the risk by 60 to 65% compared with nerve stimulation alone.1

Injection

Aspirate, then inject slowly

Aliquots of less than 5 mL with a 30 to 45 second pause between them.1

Monitoring

Watch for 30 minutes

Oxygen, monitoring and intravenous access, continued for at least 30 minutes after injection.1

In a prospective audit of more than 7,000 peripheral blocks, systemic toxicity occurred in 0.98 per 1,000 blocks.3
