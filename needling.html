Needling techniques

In plane, out of plane, and keeping the tip in view.

In plane

The needle travels along the beam

The whole shaft and the tip can be seen as a bright line, provided the needle stays within a beam that is about as thin as the needle itself.1

  • Shaft and tip both visible.
  • Longer path through tissue.
  • Small movements of the probe lose the needle.
Out of plane

The needle crosses the beam

The needle appears as a bright dot where it crosses the beam. The dot may be the shaft or the tip, and they look the same.1

  • Shorter path to the target.
  • The tip must be tracked by moving the probe or the needle.

Try it

Steepen the needle and watch it fade

At a shallow angle the shaft reflects sound back to the probe and reverberation appears beneath it. As the angle steepens, the echoes are deflected away.1

Schematic teaching image drawn by the site. It is not a patient scan.

Finding the tip

When you cannot see the needle

Stop advancing. Then work through these, which are drawn from a review of needle visualisation.1

Probe

Move the probe, not the needle

Slide, rotate or tilt the probe until the beam lies over the needle again.

Angle

Flatten the approach

Enter the skin further from the probe so that the needle lies closer to parallel with the probe face.

Tissue

Watch the tissue move

Small in-and-out movements of the needle displace the tissue around the tip.

Fluid

Hydrolocation

A small injection of fluid opens a dark pocket at the tip.

Needle

Choose the needle

Echogenic needles are designed to reflect more sound at steep angles.

Rule

No tip, no injection

If the tip cannot be located, do not advance and do not inject.
