Iliopsoas plane block

A small volume of local anaesthetic placed in front of the hip joint, between the deep surface of iliopsoas and the iliofemoral ligament. It was designed to reach the articular branches of the femoral nerve to the hip while leaving its motor branches alone.1 In volunteers it did not weaken knee extension,2 and after hip arthroscopy it cut opioid use in recovery by more than half.3 Added to a full multimodal regimen after hip arthroplasty, it made no measurable difference.4 It does not reach the obturator branches or the skin.

Level of difficulty

Intermediate block

The plane is narrow and deep. On pelvic MRI in 49 adults it measured a mean 10.7 mm across and lay 48.5 mm beneath the skin.8 A low-frequency curvilinear probe is used.59 The difficulty lies in placing the tip precisely: in the original cadaver work, 4 of 15 injections went into the iliopectineal bursa by mistake,1 and in a later cadaver study the femoral nerve lay a mean 0.8 cm from the needle track.7 The authors of that study stressed that proficiency in ultrasound-guided regional anaesthesia matters.7

The block was developed in Denmark by Nielsen, Bendtsen and colleagues, and this page uses the name they gave it.101 The 2024 ASRA–ESRA consensus on limb block nomenclature proposes naming blocks by the nerve and the anatomical location of the injection.11

Anatomy

The nerves of the front of the hip. Dissection of 13 hemipelves found femoral and obturator branches to the anterior capsule in every specimen, and accessory obturator branches in 7. High femoral branches, arising above the inguinal ligament, passed between the anterior inferior iliac spine (AIIS) and the iliopubic eminence, and in 12 the femoral supply came from these high branches alone; the accessory obturator nerve crossed the iliopubic eminence.12 A review of 21 anatomical papers located the densest nociceptive supply in the upper anterior capsule and the labrum, supplied mainly by the femoral and obturator nerves, with the nerve to quadratus femoris supplying the posterior capsule.13 A meta-analysis of dissection studies stressed how variable this supply is and gave the nerve to quadratus femoris and the obturator nerve the main share.14 The iliopsoas plane block aims at only one part of this supply: the femoral articular branches.1

The plane. Below the AIIS, iliopsoas runs over the front of the femoral head. Its deep surface lies on the iliofemoral ligament, the thick anterior part of the capsule. The original description placed the injection between the muscle and the ligament,1 and MRI in volunteers showed the injectate held in a closed fascial compartment between the parts of iliopsoas that arise inside and outside the pelvis, in front of the joint.2 At this level iliopsoas has four parts, from lateral to medial: lateral iliacus fibres, medial iliacus fibres, the iliopsoas tendon and psoas major fibres. A small muscle, iliacus minor (iliocapsularis), appears beneath iliopsoas from the level of the AIIS downwards.6 It was present in all 38 bodies in one dissection series, arising from the lower border of the AIIS, overlying the anteromedial capsule and supplied by a thin femoral branch.15

The iliopectineal bursa lies against the tendon and the front of the capsule. A review of the anatomy notes that around 15% communicate with the hip joint.6 An injection into it does not stay in the plane: it was the commonest way the cadaver injections went astray.1

How it differs from the PENG block. The PENG block places its injection on bone, between the AIIS and the iliopubic eminence, beneath the psoas tendon.1617 The iliopsoas plane block is a little further down, over the femoral head, with the tip lateral to the tendon and roughly a quarter of the PENG volume.6 The two injection points are close, and both deposit local anaesthetic deep to iliopsoas.6 Imaging and cryosection studies of the PENG block also place most of its injectate inside the iliopsoas compartment,1819 so the two blocks may share more of their mechanism than their names suggest.

Surface anatomy

The patient lies supine. The probe starts transversely just below the anterior superior iliac spine, is turned 20 to 30 degrees anticlockwise (on the right side) so that it runs along the inguinal ligament, and is slid medially until the femoral head is seen dipping beneath the acetabular rim.65 In a cadaver study that used this plane, the injection was made a mean 6.7 cm below the anterior superior iliac spine and 1.8 cm medial to it.7 The needle passes through sartorius and iliopsoas to reach the plane.5

Indications

  • Hip arthroplasty, when early mobilisation matters. Against a femoral nerve block, pain was the same, quadriceps strength better and the first time out of bed earlier.5 Quality of recovery at 24 hours was higher in a second trial against femoral block, by a median 9 points on the QoR-15.20 Against sham, on top of buprenorphine, dexamethasone, nefopam, paracetamol and celecoxib, it added nothing measurable.4
  • Hip arthroscopy. It reduced opioid use in the first three hours after surgery against placebo, without improving pain scores, nausea or ambulation.3
  • Hip fracture. In a case series of eight patients, combined with a lateral femoral cutaneous block, pain on hip flexion fell from a median 8 to 2 within half an hour.21 Peripheral nerve blocks in general reduce pain on movement and acute confusion after hip fracture,22 but no randomised trial of this block before fracture surgery was found.
  • Acetabular fracture surgery has been reported in a letter.23
  • As one half of a two-injection block with a subpectineal obturator injection (the FRONT block), still at the cadaver and case report stage.724

Nerves blocked

The target is the group of articular branches of the femoral nerve that reach the anterior hip capsule. In 10 of 15 cadaver sides, 5 mL of dye stained all of them without reaching the motor branches.1 A scoping review counts the accessory obturator branches among the targets of the plane;6 that nerve was present in 7 of 13 dissections.12 MRI in volunteers showed minor spread into the fascia iliaca compartment without measurable weakness.26

It is not designed to reach the obturator articular branches, which need a subpectineal injection,257 the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve, which supplies the skin of a lateral or anterolateral incision,26 or the nerves to the posterior capsule.13

What the block covers

The front of the hip capsule. The skin, the medial capsule and the back of the joint need other measures.

Landmark technique

There is none. The block was described as an ultrasound-guided injection from the start, defined by the sonographic plane between iliopsoas and the iliofemoral ligament.1 This page does not describe a landmark technique.

Ultrasound technique

  1. Supine patient, low-frequency curvilinear probe, placed transversely just below the anterior superior iliac spine.5
  2. Turn the probe 20 to 30 degrees so it runs along the inguinal ligament, and slide it medially.69
  3. Stop where the femoral head dips beneath the acetabular rim. This is the level of the block.6
  4. Name the layers: sartorius superficially and laterally, iliopsoas with its tendon, the bright iliofemoral ligament over the femoral head, and the femoral nerve and artery medially.5 Run colour Doppler along the planned path.
  5. Aim for the plane between iliopsoas and the iliofemoral ligament, lateral to the tendon.16
  6. Inject and watch. The fluid should spread beneath iliopsoas. Fluid that collects medially around the tendon suggests the iliopectineal bursa; reposition before giving more.16
  7. Add a lateral femoral cutaneous nerve block if the incision is lateral or anterolateral; both arthroplasty trials did so in every patient.520

Cadaver

Where 5 mL goes

In 15 cadaver sides, the dye stayed in the plane and stained every femoral articular branch, without the motor branches, in 10 (67%). In 4 (27%) the injection went into the iliopectineal bursa and spread from there, and in 1 adhesions partly blocked the spread.1

RCT, volunteers

Strength preserved

Twenty volunteers received paired, blinded active and sham blocks: 5 mL of lidocaine 1.8% with adrenaline against saline. Knee extension force changed by a mean −9.7 N (95% CI −22 to 3.0, not significant) in the 14 analysed. MRI placed the injectate in a closed compartment in front of the joint.2

Review

Lateral to the tendon

A scoping review of both blocks concluded that the iliopsoas plane block preserves quadriceps strength, whereas weakness after PENG reached 25% or more in recent studies. It attributed the difference to the PENG tip lying directly under the tendon, where it can enter the bursa, and to the larger PENG volume.6

Imaging study

How deep and at what angle

On MRI in 49 East Asian adults, just below the indirect tendon of rectus femoris, the plane was 10.7 mm wide and 48.5 mm deep, and the needle-to-beam angle was 84.2 degrees. The plane was wider in older patients.8

Needle path and nerve stimulator

  1. In plane, through sartorius and iliopsoas, to the plane over the iliofemoral ligament.5
  2. Keep the femoral nerve in view. In cadavers it lay a mean 0.8 cm from the needle track, and the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve 3.7 cm.7
  3. Stop on the ligament. Do not advance into the capsule.
  4. Aspirate, then inject in small aliquots, checking that the spread stays lateral to the tendon and beneath the muscle.6

Nerve stimulation

The target is a fascial plane, not a nerve, so a stimulator is not used to find it. A quadriceps twitch during needling means the tip is close to the femoral nerve. This is a clinical consideration, not the finding of a specific study.

Cadaver

Two deposits, one needle

In the FRONT technique the needle places dye first in the iliopsoas plane and then in the subpectineal compartment. Both the femoral rami and the obturator trunk were covered in 86.7% of 15 cadaver sides.7 Two of the medial injections entered the adductor compartment.24

Review

The bursa and the joint

A bursal injection can rupture the bursa and spread forwards and medially to the femoral nerve in the fascia iliaca compartment.6 Because some bursae communicate with the joint, part of the injectate may also enter the hip.6

Registry

Ultrasound and toxicity

In 25,336 peripheral nerve blocks, ultrasound guidance was associated with a lower incidence of local anaesthetic systemic toxicity.27

Volumes studied. The block was designed for 5 mL: 5 mL of dye in cadavers1 and 5 mL of lidocaine 1.8% with adrenaline in volunteers.2 Clinical studies have used 10 mL: ropivacaine 0.5% in two arthroplasty trials against femoral block,520 ropivacaine 0.75% with adrenaline 1 in 200,000 against sham,4 and ropivacaine 0.5% or 0.375% with dexamethasone 5 mg in case series.219 The authors of one trial speculated that 10 mL might be more than needed, because a few patients may have had slightly weaker thigh muscles.20 These are the volumes the studies used, not a dosing recommendation.

Dose. Work out the maximum safe dose of local anaesthetic for each patient on lean body weight,28 and add in everything else given in the same period: the lateral femoral cutaneous block, any obturator injection and the surgeon’s infiltration. Dose per weight and patient weight both predicted toxicity in a registry of 25,336 blocks,27 and small size and sarcopenia, common in older hip patients, add to the risk.29 If toxicity occurs, stop injecting, call for help, secure the airway and give oxygen, treat seizures promptly and give 20% lipid emulsion early,28 following the Association of Anaesthetists guideline.

Continuous techniques

No study of a catheter in the iliopsoas plane was found in PubMed when this page was compiled; the published clinical studies are of single injections. For longer analgesia after hip surgery, see the continuous techniques on the PENG and fascia iliaca pages.

Contraindications

  • Patient refusal, infection at the site, or allergy to local anaesthetic.
  • Anticoagulation. The ESAIC/ESRA guideline applies drug time intervals to deep, non-compressible peripheral blocks, but not to superficial, compressible ones.30 This plane lies about 5 cm deep, beneath iliopsoas and close to the femoral vessels;8 decide locally which category it falls into.
  • When the main pain source lies elsewhere, for example a lateral incision or the medial capsule, the block will not be enough on its own.2625

These are standard clinical considerations rather than findings of a specific study, except where referenced.

Side effects and complications

Spread outside the plane is the problem that matters most for this block. Tap a bar for the detail.

These are small cadaver studies with different methods and volumes. Compare within a study, not across them.

  • Quadriceps weakness. Not significant in volunteers,2 and better preserved than after femoral nerve block in two trials.520 Against sham, quadriceps strength did not differ.4 Minor spread into the fascia iliaca compartment has been seen on MRI.6
  • Falls. In the arthroplasty trial against femoral block, there were no in-hospital falls after this block and one after femoral block.5 A meta-analysis of 31 trials found that single-shot femoral nerve block increased falls (risk ratio 3.62; absolute increase 0.9%), and more falls than non-motor techniques, including the iliopsoas plane block, considered together (risk ratio 2.75).33
  • Bursal or intra-articular injection, the commonest misplacement in cadavers.16
  • Nerve injury, vascular injury and infection. None occurred in either group of 45 patients in the arthroplasty trial.5 The Cochrane review of blocks for hip fracture found low risks of permanent injury.22 The numbers are too small to give a rate for this block.
  • Local anaesthetic systemic toxicity. 0.87 per 1000 peripheral nerve blocks in the Australasian registry, lower with ultrasound.27 Toxicity now tends to present later than it used to.29 These figures cover all peripheral blocks, not this one alone.

Pearls of wisdom

  • Find the femoral head dipping beneath the acetabular rim. If it is not on the screen, you are at the wrong level.6
  • Stay lateral to the tendon. Fluid pooling around the tendon is the bursa, not the plane.16
  • The point of this block is its small volume. It was built on 5 mL;12 the clinical trials used 10 mL.54
  • Always think about the skin. Both arthroplasty trials added a lateral femoral cutaneous block,520 and in volunteers a lateral femoral cutaneous block rarely numbed the skin over the greater trochanter.26
  • It does not touch the obturator branches. A subpectineal obturator block reduced opioid use after hip arthroscopy but weakened the adductors,34 and in an earlier trial an obturator nerve block did not reduce opioid use after hip arthroplasty.35
  • Do not expect it to show on top of a strong multimodal regimen.4 Its clearest benefit is during the short, sharp pain after hip arthroscopy.3
  • The femoral nerve is less than a centimetre from the needle track. Keep it in view.7

Evolving evidence

Watch this space Where practice is moving, and what has not yet been settled.

Does it add anything to good multimodal analgesia?

Against sham, on top of a strong regimen, it did not.4 Against femoral block, it matched analgesia with better strength.520 Two 2026 reviews call the evidence limited and mixed.3740 A placebo-controlled arthroplasty trial with a standard UK analgesic regimen would answer the question.

The FRONT block

One needle, two deposits: the iliopsoas plane and the subpectineal compartment, aiming at the whole anterior capsule.7 A 2026 review found anatomical logic and early case reports, but no evidence yet that it improves analgesia or spares both quadriceps and adductors.24

PENG and the iliopsoas plane: one compartment?

Imaging in patients and cryosections in cadavers place PENG injectate mostly inside the iliopsoas compartment, with little on the capsule.1819 If the two blocks act in the same place, the volume may matter more than the name. A direct trial is still awaited.

Falls and early mobilisation

The case against femoral nerve block for hip surgery has hardened with a 2026 meta-analysis of falls.33 Motor-sparing blocks of the hip capsule are increasingly used to support fast-track pathways.41 A retrospective study of 592 older patients associated the block with earlier mobilisation, with no difference in pain at 6 months.42
