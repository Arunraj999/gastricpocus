Does it work

Diagnostic performance

QuestionWhat the evidence shows
Can it detect a full stomach?Sensitivity 1.0 and specificity 0.975 in 80 blinded sessions with a 50% pre-test probability.1
Are the measurements reproducible?Intra-rater and inter-rater reliability of the antral area were high among trained anaesthetists; free tracing and two diameters gave equivalent results.2
Does area predict volume?A model from 108 patients, with fluid suctioned under gastroscopy, predicts volume from right lateral antral area and age, for 0 to 500 mL.3
Does a simple grade work?In 200 fasted patients, the 0 to 2 antral grade tracked predicted volume: grade 2 identified the larger volumes.4

Who has a full stomach

Prevalence

GroupWhat the evidence shows
Fasted, elective6.2% (32 of 538) had a full stomach; only six of the 32 had a recognised risk factor.5
EmergencyA full stomach in 56% of emergency and 5% of elective cases among 440 patients. Obesity, diabetes, emergency surgery and pre-operative opioids were independent predictors.6
Children, elective1% of 200 fasted children had an at-risk stomach.17
Children, appendicitis32% of 115 children had higher-risk contents; 13% had solid or thick fluid.19
Term pregnancy, fastedGastric volume was similar to non-pregnant women; 95th centile 115 mL, 1.4 mL/kg.14

GLP-1 receptor agonists

The fastest-moving evidence

StudyWhat it found
Sen 2024, 124 patientsIncreased residual gastric content in 56% of patients on a once-weekly GLP-1 receptor agonist against 19% of controls.7
Nersessian 2024, 220 patientsSemaglutide taken within 10 days of surgery: increased content in 40% (43 of 107) against 3% (3 of 113) of controls. No pulmonary aspiration occurred.8
Vlaeminck 2026, 88 patients, matched for age, BMI and diabetesFull stomach in 49% on semaglutide against 18% of matched controls (odds ratio 4.29). Solid content in 42% against 7%. Calculated fluid volume did not differ.9
Pai 2026, 316 patients on GLP-1 receptor agonists36% had high residual content. Those with low content had withheld the drug for a median of 8 days and fasted from solids for a median of 20 hours; those with high content, 6 days and 15 hours.10
UK consensus 2025Advises continuing these drugs before elective surgery, with individual risk assessment and measures to reduce aspiration risk.11

Across these studies the extra risk comes mainly from solid content, which no fasting interval reliably excludes. This is the group in which a bedside scan changes most.

Special groups

Where the adult rules change

GroupWhat the evidence shows
Severe obesityPredicted and suctioned volumes agreed closely in 38 subjects with a BMI above 35.12
Pregnancy, third trimesterAn antral area of 9.6 cm² in the semi-recumbent right lateral position identified 1.5 mL/kg or more (sensitivity 80%, specificity 66.7%). A systematic review of 22 studies found the scan reliable and feasible.1315
ChildrenThe 0 to 2 grade applies; a paediatric volume model exists.16
Children, non-elective surgeryThe scan changed the planned induction in 67 of 130 children; the technique was appropriate in 85% against 49% by clinical assessment.18
Pyloric stenosisAn empty stomach was confirmed in 30 of 34 infants after aspiration, allowing a non-rapid induction in 88%.20

Practice

Training and guidance

QuestionWhat the evidence shows
How many scans to learn?About 24 supervised examinations for a 90% success rate and 33 for 95%, in qualitative assessment.21
What are its limits?Less reliable after gastric surgery; a large hiatus hernia can hide contents; inconclusive in 2 to 3% of examinations.22
What do expert bodies say?ASRA Pain Medicine published expert practice recommendations in 2026 for gastric ultrasound before regional anaesthesia and pain procedures in medically complex patients.23
