Gastric ultrasound in children

A superficial antrum, a smaller probe, and evidence strongest where it matters most: the emergency list.

Probe

Linear, 5 to 12 MHz

Below 40 kg a high-frequency probe gives better resolution of the superficial antrum.1

Position

Supine and right lateral

In healthy children the antral area correlated with gastric volume best in the right lateral position, in two MRI studies.34

Feasibility

94% supine, 99% right lateral

The antrum was measured in 94% of 100 fasted children in the supine position and 99% in the right lateral position.2

Interpretation

Grade first; treat calculated volumes with care

  • The 0 to 2 grade applies. In 100 children aged 11 months to 18 years, a rising antral grade went with a rising suctioned gastric volume.2
  • Volume model. A model using right lateral antral area and age was the best fit (R² = 0.60).2 It is quoted as: volume (mL) = −7.8 + 3.5 × right lateral area (cm²) + 0.127 × age (months), validated for ages 1 to 18 years and volumes up to 1.5 mL/kg.1
  • Precision. Direct calculation of volume from area is imprecise in children; the scan works best as a risk test.34
  • Thresholds used in studies. Any solid or thick fluid, or clear fluid above 0.8 mL/kg (emergency studies)67 or above 1.25 mL/kg (elective cohort).5

Evidence

What the cohorts found

Elective surgery

1% at risk

Of 200 children fasted for elective surgery, none had solid content and two had more than 1.25 mL/kg: an at-risk stomach in 1%.5

Non-elective surgery

Plan changed in 67 of 130

Ultrasound was feasible in 130 of 143 children and changed the planned induction in 67. An appropriate technique was used in 85%, against 49% from clinical assessment alone.6

Acute appendicitis

32% higher risk

Of 115 children, 37 had higher-risk gastric contents, including 15 with solid or thick fluid.7

Pyloric stenosis

Empty in 30 of 34

In infants for pyloromyotomy, ultrasound confirmed an empty stomach in 30 of 34 after gastric tube aspiration, allowing a non-rapid induction in 88%.8

A scoping review of 70 studies describes wider uses in children, including gastric tube position and gastric insufflation.9
