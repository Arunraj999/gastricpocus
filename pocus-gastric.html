Gastric POCUS

A two-minute bedside scan that shows whether the stomach is empty, holds clear fluid, or holds solid. It answers the question the fasting history cannot.

Why scan

Fasted is not always empty

Elective

6.2% full

Of 538 fasted elective patients, 32 had a full stomach on ultrasound: 9 with solid and 23 with clear fluid above 1.5 mL/kg. Only six of the 32 had a recognised risk factor.8

Emergency

56% full

In 440 consecutive patients a full stomach was found in 56% of emergency cases and 5% of elective ones. Obesity, diabetes, emergency surgery and pre-operative opioids were independent predictors.9

Accuracy

Sensitivity 1.0

In a blinded study of 80 sessions with a 50% pre-test probability, gastric ultrasound detected a full stomach with a sensitivity of 1.0 and a specificity of 0.975.6

The framework

I-AIM: four steps

Indication, Acquisition, Interpretation, Medical decision-making.4

I

Indication

Fasting status unknown or unreliable, emergency surgery, delayed gastric emptying, or a patient taking a GLP-1 receptor agonist.45

A

Acquisition

Curved low-frequency probe in most adults. Scan the antrum supine or semi-recumbent, then in the right lateral position.5

I

Interpretation

Name the content: empty, clear fluid, or thick fluid and solid. If clear fluid, measure the volume.35

M

Medical decision

Combine the scan with the patient and the operation. The scan informs the plan; it does not make it.4

Acquisition

How to scan

  1. Use a curved low-frequency probe on an abdominal preset. A linear probe can be enough in thin adults and children.5
  2. Place the probe in the epigastrium in a sagittal plane, just below the xiphisternum, and slide from left to right.5
  3. Find the antrum: a hollow viscus lying between the left lobe of the liver in front and the pancreas behind, at the level of the aorta or the inferior vena cava.35
  4. Scan supine or semi-recumbent first, then turn the patient to the right lateral position. Fluid and solid move to the antrum under gravity, so the right lateral view is the more sensitive.15
  5. For volume, measure the antral cross-sectional area in the right lateral position, between contractions, including the full thickness of the wall.25
  6. Use either free tracing or two perpendicular diameters; the two methods agree, and trained anaesthetists reproduce the measurement closely.7

From scan to decision

Solid, thick or particulate content of any amount, or clear fluid above 1.5 mL/kg, is treated as a full stomach. An empty antrum, or clear fluid at or below 1.5 mL/kg, is consistent with a fasted stomach.68

Special groups

Where the fasting history helps least

GLP-1 receptor agonists

56% against 19%

In 124 fasted patients, increased residual gastric content was found in 56% of those taking a once-weekly GLP-1 receptor agonist and 19% of controls.13 The UK multidisciplinary consensus advises continuing these drugs before surgery, with risk assessment and techniques to reduce aspiration risk.14

Severe obesity

Model holds

In 38 subjects with a BMI above 35, predicted and suctioned volumes agreed closely, with a mean difference of 35 mL.11

Third trimester

9.6 cm² cut-off

In 60 non-labouring women, an antral area of 9.6 cm² in the semi-recumbent right lateral position identified volumes of 1.5 mL/kg or more with a sensitivity of 80% and a specificity of 66.7%. The adult formula does not apply.12

Emergency surgery

Scan everyone

The authors of the 440-patient cohort concluded that gastric ultrasound should be performed in all emergency patients, and in elective patients with predictive factors.9

Area alone

340 mm² cut-off

A supine antral area of 340 mm² diagnosed an at-risk stomach, defined as solid or more than 0.8 mL/kg, with a sensitivity of 91% and a specificity of 71%.10

Children

Separate model

A paediatric model was derived from endoscopically suctioned volumes; the adult formula should not be used in children.15

Strengths and weaknesses

What the scan does well, and where it fails

Advantages
  • At the bedside, non-invasive, no radiation. Done in the anaesthetic room with the monitors attached, in about two and a half minutes.
  • Accurate. Sensitivity 1.0 and specificity 0.975 for a full stomach in a blinded study.6
  • Reproducible. Trained anaesthetists reproduce the antral measurement closely.7
  • Works in difficult groups. The antrum was seen in more than 90% of patients with a BMI above 40, and the scan is feasible in pregnancy and in children.16
  • Changes the plan. In non-elective paediatric surgery it changed the planned induction in 67 of 130 children, and the technique was appropriate in 85% against 49% from clinical assessment alone.18
  • Learnable. About 33 supervised scans for a 95% success rate in telling empty from fluid from solid.17
Disadvantages and pitfalls
  • Previous gastric surgery. The scan is less reliable when the anatomy has been altered, for example after gastric banding, sleeve gastrectomy, gastric bypass or fundoplication.16
  • Large hiatus hernia. Contents can sit in the herniated part of the stomach, out of reach of the probe, so the risk is underestimated.16
  • Sometimes inconclusive. In 2 to 3% of patients the antrum cannot be identified with confidence: gas, unusual anatomy, or bowel mistaken for stomach.16
  • Supine alone can mislead. Small volumes pool in the fundus. A stomach is only called empty after the right lateral view.16
  • The volume formula has limits. Validated for 0 to 500 mL in non-pregnant adults with a BMI below 40. It does not apply in pregnancy or in children.2
  • Operator dependent. It needs training and supervised practice before the result can be relied on.17
  • It shows content, not risk. The scan does not measure sphincter tone, reflux or airway reflexes. It informs the plan; it does not replace judgement.4

Limits of the scan

  • The adult volume model was validated for 0 to 500 mL in non-pregnant adults with a BMI below 40.2
  • Studies excluded patients with altered gastric anatomy, such as previous gastric surgery.13
  • In one cohort the examination was inconclusive in 20 of 538 patients.8
  • The scan is used when the risk of aspiration is uncertain or unknown; it adds to clinical judgement and does not replace it.8
