Ultrasound physics

What the machine is doing while you look for the nerve. Watch the film, then try the controls yourself.

1 min 36 s · animated, with on-screen captions · part 1 of the Basics series

The essentials

Six ideas that explain the picture

01

Pulse and echo

The probe sends a short pulse and then listens. The machine assumes a speed of sound of 1540 m/s in soft tissue and places each echo by the time it took to return: depth = speed × time ÷ 2.1

02

Frequency

Higher frequency gives a shorter wavelength and better resolution, but is attenuated sooner. Use the highest frequency that still reaches the target.1

03

Echogenicity

Echoes form where tissues differ in acoustic impedance. Strong reflectors appear bright (hyperechoic), weak ones dark (hypoechoic), and fluid black (anechoic).1

04

Anisotropy

Nerves and tendons reflect best when the beam meets them at 90°. A small tilt of the probe can make a nerve fade or reappear.2

05

Attenuation

Sound loses energy with depth, more so at higher frequencies. Time-gain compensation amplifies the later echoes to even out the image.1

06

Artefacts

Acoustic shadowing behind bone, enhancement behind fluid and reverberation beneath a needle all arise when tissue breaks the machine's assumptions.2
