Gastric ultrasound: the combined summary

Everything a trainee needs on one page: who to scan, how to scan, what you will see, how to measure and what to do next. Built from the consensus statements, systematic reviews and key studies, and set out in the I-AIM order: indication, acquisition, interpretation, medical decision-making.1

Six things to remember

The question

Is there solid food, thick fluid or more than 1.5 mL/kg of clear fluid in the stomach? If so, treat it as a full stomach.23

The view

Sagittal plane in the epigastrium: liver above, antrum in cross-section, pancreas behind it, aorta or IVC deeper still.34

Two positions

Supine or semi-recumbent first, then right lateral. Only call a stomach empty after the right lateral view.35

Measure

Right lateral, between contractions, serosa to serosa, mean of three readings.26

Learning

About 33 supervised scans for 95% success in telling empty, fluid and solid apart.7

Its place

The scan informs the plan. An inconclusive scan is not an empty stomach.2

1. Indication: who to scan

The scan earns its place when the fasting history cannot be relied on, or when a condition slows gastric emptying. The American Society of Regional Anesthesia and Pain Medicine reviewed seven groups in 2026.8

Recommendation (ASRA Pain Medicine 2026)Patient groups
SupportedActive labour; urgent caesarean section; diabetes
Conditional supportObesity; emergency care; enteral feeding; GLP-1 receptor agonists
Not recommended routinelyPregnancy not in labour; elective caesarean section; gastro-oesophageal reflux disease

Other situations named in the consensus statements: uncertain or unknown fasting status, a broken fast, trauma, opioid treatment and emergency surgery.29

2. Acquisition: how to scan

ProbeLow-frequency curved array on an abdominal preset, for adults and larger children. A linear high-frequency probe gives a better picture in smaller children, below about 30 to 40 kg.23
PositionSupine or 45° semi-recumbent first, then turn to the right lateral decubitus position, which moves the contents into the antrum. In late pregnancy avoid lying flat: scan semi-recumbent at 45°, then semi-recumbent right lateral.210
PlaneSagittal or parasagittal in the epigastrium, just below the xiphisternum, marker to the head. Sweep across the epigastrium until the landmarks line up.34
LandmarksLeft lobe of the liver anteriorly, the antrum beneath it, the pancreas behind, and the aorta (or the inferior vena cava) in long axis.34
MeasuringFreeze in the right lateral position between peristaltic contractions, when the antrum is largest. Trace the outer edge of the serosa, or multiply the anteroposterior and craniocaudal diameters by π/4. Take the mean of three readings.26

3. Interpretation: what you will see

The antral wall

The wall shows the "gut signature" of the bowel. Five layers can be seen with a high-frequency probe: from the lumen outwards, a bright mucosal interface, dark mucosa, bright submucosa, dark muscularis propria (the thickest) and bright serosa. With a low-frequency curved probe in adults it usually appears as three bands: an inner bright band (mucosa and submucosa), a dark muscularis propria and an outer bright band (serosa and the fat around it).2 The volume models were built on full-thickness measurements, so the area is always traced at the outer edge of the serosa.211

ContentUltrasound appearance
EmptySmall, flat antrum with thick walls; the walls lie together, giving a target or "bull's eye".
Clear fluidRound, thin-walled, dark (anechoic) antrum; swallowed air bubbles can give a "starry night".
Thick fluidMilk and similar fluids are evenly bright (hyperechoic).
Solid, earlyA bright front edge with air mixed into the food: "frosted glass", which hides the back wall and deeper structures.
Solid, laterMixed, patchy (heterogeneous) content in a distended antrum, once the air has gone.

Measuring the volume

Non-pregnant adults: volume (mL) = 27.0 + 14.6 × right lateral area (cm²) − 1.28 × age (years), validated from 0 to 500 mL with a BMI below 40.11 It also held in severely obese subjects.12 Use the calculator and the Perlas nomogram, or the cut-off table below.

Patient groups at a glance

AdultsCurved probe; adult volume model; 1.5 mL/kg threshold.112
Severe obesityVolume model held (BMI above 35, 38 subjects). Inconclusive in up to 5%.122
Pregnancy, third trimesterSemi-recumbent; adult formula does not apply. Right lateral area of 9.6 cm² identified 1.5 mL/kg or more (sensitivity 80%, specificity 66.7%). Systematic review of 22 studies: reliable and feasible.1013
ChildrenLinear probe below about 30 to 40 kg; 0 to 2 grade applies; paediatric volume model available; 1% of fasted elective children had an at-risk stomach.21415
GLP-1 receptor agonistsResidual content in 40 to 56% against 3 to 19% of controls; the excess is mainly solid. UK consensus: continue the drug and assess risk individually.16171819
Emergency surgeryFull stomach in 56% of emergency against 5% of elective cases.9
Emergency physiciansSystematic review of nine studies: adults sensitivity 86 to 100%, specificity 70 to 92%; children less reliable (specificity 22 to 74%); low-quality evidence.20

How good is it, and where does it fail

AccuracySensitivity 1.0 and specificity 0.975 for a full stomach in a blinded study.21
ReproducibilityHigh intra- and inter-rater reliability of the antral area among trained anaesthetists.6
LimitsLess reliable after gastric surgery; a large hiatus hernia can hide contents; inconclusive in 2 to 3% of examinations, up to 5% in severe obesity.32
LearningAbout 24 supervised scans for 90% and 33 for 95% success.7
What is not knownThe 1.5 mL/kg threshold is a pragmatic, model-derived cut-off; a direct link to aspiration has not been shown.2

Where the sources agree, and where they differ

Agree

  • Solid content, or clear fluid at 1.5 mL/kg or more, means a full stomach.23
  • Right lateral view before calling the stomach empty; serosa-to-serosa area between contractions.23
  • The scan supports, and does not replace, clinical judgement.82

Differ

  • Which patients should be scanned routinely: ASRA supports labour, urgent caesarean section and diabetes, and only conditionally obesity, emergencies and GLP-1 drugs.8
  • GLP-1 receptor agonists: the UK consensus advises continuing them with individual assessment, while other society advice has ranged from holding the drug to a symptom-based approach.192
  • The evidence for emergency physician use and for children is weaker.20
