The blocks

What each one covers

  • Thoracic paravertebral block. Reduces pain scores, analgesic use and postoperative nausea and vomiting after breast surgery; a catheter can continue it after the operation.14
  • PECS II. Two injections, in the interpectoral plane and the pectoserratus plane. It aims to block the pectoral nerves, the intercostobrachial nerve, the third to sixth intercostal nerves and the long thoracic nerve.59
  • Serratus anterior plane block. Described in volunteers as a block of the lateral chest wall; clinical trials were called for by its authors.6
  • Erector spinae plane block. Reduced opioid use and opioid side effects, but the analgesic gain over systemic analgesia was judged not clinically important.7
The arithmetic

How the limit is worked out

  • Maximum dose is the lower of the weight-based figure and the ceiling for the drug, the same limits as the dose calculator.10
  • Dosing weight is actual body weight up to a BMI of 40. At a BMI of 40 or more it is lean body weight, estimated from sex, weight and height.1112
  • Maximum volume in mL is the maximum dose in mg divided by ten times the concentration in per cent.
  • Every injection counts: each block, both sides if bilateral, the donor site, and the surgeon's infiltration.
  • These limits are not trial-derived, and fascial plane blocks are absorbed readily. Treat the result as an upper boundary.10
