Gastric ultrasound: how to scan

A four-minute animated tutorial: probe, position, landmarks, what the antrum looks like, grading, measuring and the decision.

Transcript

What the video says

0:00 · Gastric ultrasound

A bedside scan that takes about two and a half minutes and answers one question. Is this stomach empty?

0:11 · Why scan

A fasting history is not always reliable. Of 538 fasted elective patients, 6.2% had a full stomach on ultrasound. In another study, a full stomach was found in 56% of emergency cases.

0:32 · Set up

Adults: curved low-frequency probe (1 to 5 MHz), abdominal preset. Children under 40 kg: linear high-frequency probe (5 to 12 MHz). Start supine or semi-recumbent.

0:50 · Probe position

Epigastrium, just below the xiphisternum, sagittal plane. Perpendicular to the skin, marker towards the head. Sweep across from the left costal margin.

1:06 · Find the antrum

Find the antrum. Left lobe of the liver: its lower edge. The antrum, in cross-section. Behind it, the pancreas. Deeper: the aorta in long axis, in front of the spine. Standard plane: the antrum at the level of the aorta.

1:32 · Right lateral position

Turn the patient onto the right side. Gravity moves the contents to the antrum: the more sensitive view. Only call a stomach empty after looking in this position. If turning is not practical: semi-recumbent.

1:53 · What you see

Empty: small, flat, thick-walled. The bull’s eye. Clear fluid: round, thin-walled, dark inside. Air bubbles give a starry night. Thick fluid, such as milk: uniformly bright. Solid, early: frosted glass that hides the deeper structures. Solid, later: mixed bright content in a distended antrum.

2:28 · Grade clear fluid

When the content is clear fluid, grade it. Grade 0: empty in both positions. Grade 1: fluid only in the right lateral position. Low volume. Grade 2: fluid in both positions. Likely 1.5 mL/kg or more.

2:51 · Measure the volume

Freeze in the right lateral position, between contractions. Trace the serosa all the way round. Mean of three readings. Volume (mL) = 27 + 14.6 × area (cm²) − 1.28 × age (years).

3:19 · From scan to decision

Empty, or clear fluid below 1.5 mL/kg: consistent with a fasted stomach. Clear fluid at or above 1.5 mL/kg, or any thick fluid or solid: full stomach. The scan informs the plan. You decide with the whole patient in mind.

3:42 · Learning the scan

How many scans does it take to learn? About 33 supervised examinations for a 95% success rate (Arzola 2013). Start counting.
