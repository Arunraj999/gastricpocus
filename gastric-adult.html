Draft for clinical sign-off. Compiled 20 November 2026 by Dr Arun Mohanraj FRCA EDRA, Wrightington, Wigan and Leigh NHS Foundation Trust.

Probe

Curved, 1 to 5 MHz

Standard abdominal preset. The antrum is superficial enough in slim adults for a linear probe.1

Position

Supine, then right lateral

The right lateral position moves the contents to the antrum and is the more sensitive. Semi-recumbent is a reasonable alternative when turning is impractical.1

Plane

Sagittal, epigastric

Just below the xiphisternum, at the level of the aorta, with the antrum between the left lobe of the liver and the pancreas.1

Interpretation

Grade, then measure

GradeSupineRight lateralMeaning
0EmptyEmptyEmpty stomach. Low risk.
1EmptyFluid seenVolume below 1.5 mL/kg. Low risk.
2Fluid seenFluid seenVolume likely 1.5 mL/kg or more. Higher risk.

Grading applies to clear fluid. Thick fluid or solid is a full stomach whatever the amount.12

  • Volume. Volume (mL) = 27 + 14.6 × right lateral antral area (cm²) − 1.28 × age (years). Trace the serosa between contractions and take the mean of three readings.13
  • Threshold. Clear fluid below 1.5 mL/kg of actual body weight is consistent with baseline secretions, about 100 to 130 mL in an average adult.1
  • Accuracy. With a 50% pre-test probability, the scan detected a full stomach with a sensitivity of 1.0 and a specificity of 0.975.4

Who benefits

Where the fasting history helps least

Fasted elective

6.2% full

Of 538 fasted elective patients, 32 had a full stomach on ultrasound.5

Emergency

56% full

A full stomach was found in 56% of emergency cases and 5% of elective cases among 440 consecutive patients.6

GLP-1 receptor agonists

56% against 19%

Increased residual gastric content in 56% of fasted patients taking a once-weekly GLP-1 receptor agonist and 19% of controls.8 UK consensus advises continuing these drugs with individual risk assessment.9

Severe obesity

Model holds

The antrum was seen in the right lateral position in more than 90% of patients with a BMI above 40.1 Predicted and suctioned volumes agreed closely in 38 subjects with a BMI above 35.7

Medically complex

ASRA 2026

Expert practice recommendations now cover gastric ultrasound before regional anaesthesia and pain procedures in medically complex patients.11

Limits

When to distrust it

Less reliable after gastric surgery. A large hiatus hernia can hide contents. Inconclusive in 2 to 3% of examinations.1

Training

How many scans to learn it

Learning curve

24 scans for 90%, 33 for 95%

Six anaesthetists each performed 30 supervised examinations after teaching and a hands-on workshop. The number needed to tell empty from clear fluid from solid was estimated at 24 for a 90% success rate and 33 for a 95% success rate.10

At the bedside

A short, focused scan

The examination answers one question and uses two positions. It fits into the anaesthetic room routine with the monitors attached. A logbook of supervised scans is the practical route to competence.
