FILM 1: ULTRASOUND PHYSICS (1 min 36 s)  file: nora2-ultrasound-physics.mp4
=====================================================================
0:00  Title. "Ultrasound physics. What the machine is doing while you look for the nerve."
0:05  Send a pulse, then listen.
      The probe is a loudspeaker and a microphone. It assumes sound travels through tissue at
      1540 metres per second, and places each echo by how long it took to return.
0:21  Frequency: detail against depth.
      Higher frequency means a shorter wavelength and a sharper picture, but the tissue absorbs it
      sooner. Use the highest frequency that still reaches your target.
0:35  Echoes come from boundaries.
      An echo forms wherever two tissues differ in acoustic impedance. The bigger the difference,
      the brighter the line. Fluid has no boundaries inside it, so it stays black.
0:49  Anisotropy: the angle matters.
      Nerves and tendons reflect like mirrors. Meet them at ninety degrees and they shine. Tilt the
      probe a few degrees and the echo misses the probe, so the nerve fades.
1:03  Attenuation and time-gain compensation.
      Sound loses energy with every centimetre, so deep echoes come back faint. Time-gain
      compensation amplifies the late echoes until the picture is evenly bright from top to bottom.
1:16  Three artefacts you will meet.
      The machine assumes sound travels in a straight line, at one speed, and returns after a single
      reflection. When tissue breaks those rules, the picture misleads: these are artefacts.
1:30  End card. "Next: Knobology."

Suggested description:    Pulse-echo imaging, frequency, echogenicity, anisotropy, attenuation and
                          artefacts, for anaesthetists learning ultrasound-guided regional anaesthesia.
                          Reference: Sites BD et al. Reg Anesth Pain Med 2007; 32: 412-8 and 419-33.

FILM 2: KNOBOLOGY (1 min 37 s)  file: nora2-knobology.mp4
=====================================================================
0:00  Title. "Knobology. Six controls to set before the needle goes in."
0:05  Choose the probe and the preset.
      Start with the probe that suits the depth of your target, then pick the nerve or
      musculoskeletal preset. The preset sets sensible starting values for everything that follows.
0:16  Depth.
      Set the depth so the target sits near the middle of the screen. Too much depth wastes the
      screen and shrinks the nerve; too little hides the structures behind it.
0:28  Frequency.
      Raise the frequency for a sharper picture of a shallow target. Lower it only when the target
      is too deep to see.
0:40  Gain and time-gain compensation.
      Gain is brightness. Set it so that fluid is black and fascia is white. Then use the time-gain
      sliders to even out the near and far fields.
0:55  Focus.
      The beam is narrowest at the focal zone, and that is where side-to-side detail is best. Where
      your machine has a focus control, place the marker level with the target.
1:06  Colour Doppler.
      Before the needle goes in, place the colour box over the target and over the path you plan to
      take. Keep the box small and avoid pressing hard, which can empty a vein.
1:17  Move the probe: PART.
      The last control is your hand. Pressure, alignment, rotation and tilt: small movements, one
      at a time, with the screen as your guide.
1:31  End card. "Next: Needling techniques."
